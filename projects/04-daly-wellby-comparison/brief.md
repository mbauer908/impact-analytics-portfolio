# Cash, psychotherapy, and bed nets on a wellbeing scale

GiveWell ranks life-saving programs far above cash transfers, per dollar. The Happier Lives Institute ranks programs by self-reported wellbeing. This note asks what that second ranking does to the same programs. It does not treat either unit as the right one.

### Decision

#### TLDR

Group psychotherapy produces about five times the wellbeing per dollar of cash transfers. Bed nets might match cash transfers or beat psychotherapy, depending on how bad a death is taken to be. Vitamin A supplementation and seasonal malaria prevention have no published wellbeing-year estimate.

The table is what the Happier Lives Institute has published, in wellbeing-years per $1,000 donated. A wellbeing-year (WELLBY) is one point on a 0–10 life-satisfaction scale, for one person, for one year. Two rows are survey results. One row is a range that moves with the value of a saved life. Two rows are blank because the Institute has not published them.

| Program | How the number was produced | WELLBYs per $1,000 |
|---|---|---:|
| GiveDirectly cash transfers | Surveys of people who received a cash transfer | 7.55 |
| StrongMinds group psychotherapy | Surveys of people treated for depression | 40 |
| Against Malaria Foundation bed nets | Deaths averted, converted under a view of how bad death is | about 7 to 90 |
| Helen Keller vitamin A supplementation | Not estimated | — |
| Malaria Consortium seasonal malaria chemoprevention | Not estimated | — |

**The cash-transfer and psychotherapy results are measured.** StrongMinds produces 40 WELLBYs per $1,000 donated. GiveDirectly produces 7.55 WELLBYs per $1,000 donated. The Happier Lives Institute’s charities page calls that 5.3 times the wellbeing per dollar of a GiveDirectly cash transfer. Both figures come from trials of those programs. They are not deaths rewritten in another unit.

**The bed-net result is a judgment call.** In the Happier Lives Institute’s 2023 overview, the low end is about 7 WELLBYs per $1,000, roughly even with cash transfers, and the high end is about 90, above group psychotherapy. The Happier Lives Institute described that span as about 1 to 11 times the effect per dollar of cash transfers. The width is the value assigned to the saved life: 0 to 247 WELLBYs per death. On the Happier Lives Institute’s current comparison page the other charities’ totals stay fixed, and the bed-net total moves. With death counted as the loss of future wellbeing, and a neutral point of 2.58, bed nets rank 5th of the 10 charities shown. Change either assumption and that place changes.

**Vitamin A and seasonal malaria prevention are absent.** The Happier Lives Institute has no WELLBY cost for Helen Keller’s vitamin A supplementation or for Malaria Consortium’s seasonal chemoprevention, and no split by country. There is no wellbeing ranking of vitamin A in Niger, seasonal chemoprevention in Sokoto, or bed nets in Guinea. The Happier Lives Institute’s review of WELLBY cost-effectiveness leaves out programs whose main effect is adding years of life.

### Terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **WELLBY** | One point on a 0–10 wellbeing scale, for one person, for one year. |
| **Neutral point** | The score at which a life is neither good nor bad for the person living it. The Happier Lives Institute’s default is 2.58. |
| **Measured** | The treated group reports higher wellbeing than the comparison group. Cash transfers and group psychotherapy are measured this way. |
| **Converted** | A death, or a year of life, is turned into WELLBYs by a view of how bad death is. Bed nets are converted this way. |

### Where the numbers come from

GiveDirectly uses 12 studies of cash transfers. The recipient and the rest of the household gain about 9.2 WELLBYs from a transfer. Delivering it costs about $1,221, which is 7.55 WELLBYs per $1,000 donated.

StrongMinds is the same kind of sum for group psychotherapy in Uganda and Zambia. One course is about 1.8 WELLBYs, at about $45 per person treated, which is 40 WELLBYs per $1,000 donated.

Bed nets use one charity-wide set of inputs, not a country result: about 0.34 deaths averted per $1,000, life satisfaction of 4.95 for the people whose lives are extended, and a life expectancy of 70. Grief is added at 7.26 WELLBYs per death. Higher income among people who do not die is about 4 WELLBYs per $1,000. How many of the saved years count depends on the view of death:

- Death takes away the wellbeing the person would have had.
- A death counts for less when the person is very young.
- Death is no loss to the person who dies, so only other people’s grief remains.

The Happier Lives Institute does not adopt one of these views.

### What not to over-read

The 7-to-90 range is from 2023. It uses GiveWell’s charity-level cost figures from that year. It is not a survey of people who received nets.

The 5th-of-10 place is the default on a live comparison page. It is not a published point estimate, and it moves when the view of death or the neutral point moves.

Multiplying every life-saving program by the same wellbeing factor would only repeat their cost per death. That is not a wellbeing estimate, and this note does not do it.
